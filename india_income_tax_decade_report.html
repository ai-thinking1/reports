Between FY 2013–14 and FY 2024–25, India's direct tax base more than doubled, net collections nearly tripled, and the cost of collecting every rupee fell to a historic low. This report visualises the full twelve-year arc of who pays, how much, from where — sourced entirely from the Central Board of Direct Taxes' Time-Series Data.

Chapter 02

The rupees
that come in.

Net direct tax collections crossed ₹22 lakh crore for the first time in FY 2024–25 — a 3.5× jump from FY 2013–14. The defining structural shift: in 2020, non-corporate (mostly individual) taxes overtook corporate taxes, and the gap has widened every year since.

Chapter 03

Bigger relative to the
economy, cheaper to collect.

The Direct Tax-to-GDP ratio in FY 2024–25 reached 6.73% — its highest level in at least 24 years, since the CBDT series begins. And the state spends just 41 paise to collect every ₹100 of tax.

The Tax Buoyancy Story

Tax buoyancy — the ratio of tax growth to GDP growth — averaged 1.39 over the last decade and hit 2.54 in FY 2021–22. A reading above 1.0 means tax revenue is growing faster than the economy: direct taxes have been a structural source of fiscal expansion, not just a passive follower.

Chapter 06

A PAN for almost every adult.

As of 31 March 2025, the Income Tax Department had allotted roughly 80 crore PAN cards — that is, about half the population. Ninety-eight per cent of these PANs are held by individuals, and the gender gap among individual PAN holders has narrowed from 65–35 to 58–42 in just five years.

Chapter 07

The middle is moving up.

When CBDT analysed individual taxpayer income ranges from AY 2013–14 to AY 2021–22, the standout finding was structural: the count of filers in the ₹5–10 lakh and ₹10–25 lakh brackets nearly quadrupled. The top 1%'s share of total declared income actually fell.

A counter-intuitive number

Between AY 2013–14 and AY 2021–22, the average gross total income of the bottom 25% of individual taxpayers rose by 58% — outpacing the 42% rise for the top 1%. The share of all individual income held by the top 1% fell from 15.9% to 14.6%. The data shows broadening, not narrowing.

About this report

Every number in this document comes from the Central Board of Direct Taxes (CBDT), Department of Revenue, Ministry of Finance, Government of India — principally the consolidated Time-Series Data (FY 2000–01 to FY 2024–25) released on 5 December 2025, supplemented by PAN Allotment Statistics (March 2024 / March 2025) and the AY-2021-22 income-distribution release.

A "taxpayer" follows the CBDT definition: any person who either filed a return of income for the relevant Assessment Year, or in whose case tax was deducted at source for the relevant Financial Year.
