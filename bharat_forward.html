Section Two · The Destination

The frame: Viksit Bharat @ 2047.

Almost every scheme and target you will read about ladders up to a single anchor: making India a developed nation by 2047, the centenary of independence. Everything else — Net Zero by 2070, the $30+ trillion economy, NIP 2.0, Gati Shakti — is in service of that one date.

An economy of $30 trillion+,
built on four pillars.

The framework targets per-capita income rising from roughly $2,600 today to $15,000–$18,000 by 2047, multidimensional poverty below 5%, universal high-quality healthcare and education, and net-zero emissions on a 2070 horizon. Achieving it requires sustained 7-8% growth and an investment rate near 35% of GDP.

Underneath this vision sit two operating mechanisms: the National Infrastructure Pipeline (NIP), which is the actual list of funded projects (now in its NIP 2.0 phase, with a new 3-year, ₹17 lakh crore PPP pipeline of 852 projects launched in early 2026), and PM Gati Shakti, the GIS-based digital platform that forces 16 ministries to plan rail, road, ports, power, and pipelines on the same map. Together they are what turns a 2047 slogan into a concrete construction schedule.

Section Two-and-a-Half · The Numbers Underneath

The macro frame, circa 2026.

The roadmap sits inside a specific moment — and the Economic Survey 2025-26 captured it bluntly. India has just turned in its strongest macroeconomic performance in decades: fastest-growing major economy for the fourth year running, multi-decade-low inflation, the fiscal deficit halved from its Covid peak, and a sovereign rating upgrade after twenty years. Then a 50% US tariff arrived. This is the year in the numbers everyone underneath the schemes is actually managing against.

Three things changed structurally in 2025 that don't fit neatly inside any single sector but reshape all of them:

Labour Codes notified, finally. In November 2025 the central government notified the four Labour Codes consolidating 29 central laws — on wages, industrial relations, social security, and occupational safety. Gig and platform workers (Zomato, Swiggy, Ola, Uber) are now formally recognised and brought under national social security. The single most consequential labour-market reform since 1991.

GST 2.0 collapsed slabs. In September 2025 the GST Council compressed the rate structure from multiple tiers to essentially two slabs, with most items shifting to the lower one. Combined with the February 2025 budget raising the no-tax income threshold to ₹12 lakh, household disposable income rose materially — and showed up almost immediately in consumption.

Two big sectors opened. Insurance was opened to 100% foreign direct investment. Nuclear power generation was opened to private players for the first time. Both were long-pending unlocks. Together with three trade deals signed in 2025 — with the UK, Oman, New Zealand — and a comprehensive India-EU FTA awaiting EU Parliament ratification, India's external posture shifted from defensive to active in a single year.

The Survey raised India's medium-term potential growth rate from 6.5% to 7.0% — the policy team's way of saying these reforms have permanently lifted what the Indian economy can do. FY27 growth is projected at 6.8–7.2%.

Section Five · The Strategic Frame

The arc from protection to indispensability.

If the sectors above are the trees, the Economic Survey 2025-26 offers a frame for the forest. Chief Economic Adviser V. Anantha Nageswaran lays out a three-stage transition that organises everything else: from old-style import substitution, through the present-day posture of strategic resilience, towards the destination of strategic indispensability — becoming so critical to global value chains that coercion against India becomes too costly to attempt.

Three stances, one direction.

This is the CEA's mental model for why a 2026 budget that looks "mostly continuity" is actually carrying a tighter logic underneath. The schemes you see in each sector are not random — they are levers in a sequence.

01 · Outgoing.

Build domestic capacity behind tariff walls. Worked briefly for upstream sectors. Fails because protected firms grow lazy and pass costs to downstream exporters. The Survey is unusually candid: "decisions about what NOT to protect can be as important as decisions about what to support." Expect calibrated rollbacks of upstream tariffs on fibres, steel, aluminium.

02 · Current.

Absorb shocks, preserve stability. Diversify trade partners (UK, Oman, NZ, EU FTAs in 2025). Build reserves (11 months import cover). Secure critical inputs (semiconductors, rare earths, defence). Necessary but not sufficient — resilience prevents collapse, it doesn't generate leverage.

03 · Destination.

Become a source of reliability others cannot easily replace. UPI exported as DPI to other countries. Semiconductors and rare-earth processing at scale. Pharmaceutical capability deepened. India's standards adopted in emerging tech. The point at which coercion becomes too costly to attempt against you.

The deeper bet: an entrepreneurial state.

The Survey's final chapters argue that India's binding constraint is no longer policy design — it is execution capacity. Borrowing from Mariana Mazzucato's framework, it pushes for a state that acts before certainty emerges (the semiconductor mission, green hydrogen are early examples), structures risk rather than avoids it, learns systematically from experimentation (state-level deregulation compacts replacing inspection-based control with trust-based compliance), and course-corrects without paralysis (Quality Control Orders that harmed downstream industries put on hold, not defended out of inertia).

The diagnosis is unflinching: "bureaucratic risk aversion, fragmented accountability, and proceduralism weaken the State's ability to act decisively." The way out is not deregulation as withdrawal but deregulation as institutional reorientation — shifting administrative effort from low-value policing toward coordination, facilitation, and problem-solving. Policy reforms matter. Process reforms matter arguably more.

"The mature mind chooses Śreya — the enduring good — over Preya — the fleeting comfort. The country stands to gain immensely when all of us embrace delayed gratification." — V. Anantha Nageswaran, citing the Katha Upanishad in the closing of the Economic Survey 2025-26.

Section Six · Your Move

You can read it. You can also shape it.

A national roadmap is only as legitimate as the citizens who can interrogate, contribute to, and benefit from it. India runs two parallel channels for civic participation — one for ideas, one for benefits. Both are underused.

If you have ideas

Contribute to the plan

The MyGov portal runs open consultations on national policy — draft bills, mission frameworks, vision documents. The Viksit Bharat 2047 portal accepts long-form submissions on every theme. NITI Aayog's "Work for Viksit Bharat" portal also collects citizen contributions. They are read by the people drafting the actual schemes.

mygov.in →
If you want benefits

Find what's yours

myScheme matches your demographics — age, income, occupation, state, social category — against every active scheme and tells you which ones you qualify for and how to apply. Most Indians are eligible for far more than they claim. Set aside 20 minutes.
